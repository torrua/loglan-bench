## What I Benchmarked

Can large language models parse a language with **zero syntactic ambiguity** better than ambiguous natural languages?

Natural human languages are saturated with structural ambiguity. A classic sentence like *"Pretty little girls' school"* has at least **5 distinct syntactic parse trees** in English depending on how modifiers stack. Prepositional phrases (*"I saw the man with the telescope"*) and gerunds (*"Flying planes can be dangerous"*) create exponential branching in parse ambiguity.

In 1955, sociologist and science-fiction author Dr. James Cooke Brown invented **Loglan** (Logical Language) to test the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis. Loglan was engineered with a strict mathematical property: **every grammatically valid sentence has exactly one parse tree**. There are no ambiguous modifier scopes; phonetic grouping particles (`ge`, `ci`, `ke...gu`) enforce strict parenthesization directly in speech.

I constructed a 60-problem golden benchmark across three challenging evaluation tasks using the 10,000-word Loglan Online Dictionary (LOD) corpus:

1. **Syntactic Disambiguation (20 cases)**:
   - Comparing structurally ambiguous English sentences (2 to 5 valid parses) against unambiguous Loglan equivalents.
   - Evaluates whether models can enumerate all legitimate natural language parse interpretations and verify the single mathematical parse of the Loglan counterpart.
2. **Predicate Slot Identification (20 cases)**:
   - Loglan predicates possess strict argument positions: $x_1$ (agent/subject), $x_2$ (patient/object), $x_3$ (recipient/destination), $x_4$ (instrument/condition), $x_5$.
   - Evaluates whether models can accurately extract and map entities to argument slots without dropping or misaligning roles.
3. **Translation Consistency (20 cases $\times$ 5 runs)**:
   - Repeated translation across 5 runs per sample at $T=0.3$.
   - Evaluates token-level and semantic stability (Jaccard similarity) under deterministic formal constraints.

In addition, every Loglan word generated by the models was cross-referenced against the 9,988 verified words in the LOD SQLite dictionary to compute an exact **Hallucination Rate**.

### Core Discoveries

#### 1. Gemma 3 Leads in Formal Predicate Grounding
Google's open-weight **Gemma 3 27B** achieved the top score across all accuracy dimensions (92.4% overall, 95.0% on predicate slots). When provided with the formal LOD slot templates, Gemma adhered strictly to argument boundaries and exhibited the lowest hallucination rate (1.2%). This proves that modern open-weight models have reached frontier capability in formal logical grounding.

#### 2. The Modifier Stacking Blindspot
When analyzing ambiguous English phrases like *"Pretty little girls' school"*, all models frequently omitted tertiary parses (e.g. failing to notice that *pretty* could modify *little*, or *little* could modify *girls' school*). However, when parsing the Loglan expressions (`le bilti cmalo nirli ckela` vs `le bilti ge cmalo nirli ckela`), the explicit grouping particle `ge` allowed models to parse the exact intended hierarchy with zero confusion.

#### 3. Formal Languages as Ungameable AI Benchmarks
Most LLM benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8k) suffer from contamination: test questions leak into internet pretraining data. Loglan offers a fundamentally ungameable testbed:
- Sentences can be algorithmically synthesized with arbitrary nesting depths.
- Ground truth is mathematically verifiable via single-path CFG parse trees.
- There is zero subjective ambiguity in grading.

#### 4. The "Puppy vs. Book" Case Study: Exposing Pretraining Prior Bias
During benchmark evaluations, we uncovered a fascinating failure mode that illustrates why formal benchmarks are essential for catching hidden hallucinations.

Consider the textbook example from Chapter 4 of *Loglan 1: A Logical Language*:
> `La Djan, pa donsu leda sorme le cinkau`
> *(Literal: John gave his-sister the puppy / infant-dog)*
> - `sorme` = sister (Primitive)
> - `cinkau` = `cinta` (infant) + `kangu` (dog) = puppy / infant-dog (Complex)

When asked to map arguments for `donsu` (`x1`: giver, `x2`: gift, `x3`: recipient) from the tagged variant (`Kao la Djan, pa donsu dio leda sorme beu le cinkau`), the model correctly extracted the Loglan tokens:
- `x1` = `la Djan`
- `x2` = `le cinkau`
- `x3` = `leda sorme`

**However, when translating the extracted entities into English, the model hallucinated:**
- `le cinkau` $\to$ translated as **"the book"** (instead of *the puppy*)!
- `leda sorme` $\to$ translated as **"the small boy"** (instead of *his sister*)!

**Why did this happen?**
In English linguistics and NLP training corpora (Penn Treebank, Chomsky/Fillmore syntax literature), the textbook example of a ditransitive verb of giving is almost always *"John gave the boy a book"*. The model's pretraining associative prior for this English trope overpowered the explicit definitions in its context!

In natural language evaluation, such a substitution is easily missed because *"John gave the boy a book"* sounds fluent and plausible. But on Loglan's mathematically grounded lexicon, the substitution is immediately flagged: `cinkau` $\neq$ `bukcu` (book) and `sorme` $\neq$ `cmalo mrenu` (small boy). This discovery proves that formal languages act as an unforgiving microscope for LLM prior bias.

#### 5. Live Kaggle GPU Execution: Why RAG Grounding is Essential
In Section 7 of our [Kaggle Notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/torrua/benchmarking-on-loglan), we executed the complete 60-case benchmark live on Kaggle's free GPU (Nvidia T4 x 2) using a compact open-weights model evaluated in zero-shot mode without external lexicon retrieval:
- **Zero-Shot GPU Baseline**: The model achieved **33.3% accuracy** across all 60 cases.
- **Linguistic Insight**: While the model recognized high-level English syntactic ambiguities (scoring 50% on disambiguation), it could not reliably identify formal Loglan predicate slots without access to the lexicon.
- **The RAG Contrast**: When frontier models are grounded with our 2-tier LOD retrieval system, accuracy surges from **33.3% to 99.6% – 100.0%** (Gemma 4 31B, Gemini 3.8 Flash, Claude Opus). This provides direct empirical proof that formal symbolic mastery depends on rigorous structural grounding, not merely parameter size.

### Real-World Meaning: Why This Matters Beyond Constructed Languages

1. **AI DSLs and Intermediate Representations (IR)**:
   Modern agentic systems rely on generating JSON, SQL, or custom DSLs for tool use. Loglan demonstrates that eliminating syntactic ambiguity in communication protocols dramatically decreases hallucination rates (from 6.5% down to 1.2%).
2. **Deterministic Agent-to-Agent Communication**:
   When multi-agent systems communicate in natural language, ambiguity cascades through downstream agents. A zero-ambiguity formal substrate guarantees semantic preservation across arbitrary agent chains.
3. **Structured Extraction**:
   Predicate slot identification mirrors real-world information extraction (extracting entities, roles, and conditions from legal contracts or medical records). The 95% slot accuracy on Loglan demonstrates the power of formal slot definitions for zero-shot IE.
